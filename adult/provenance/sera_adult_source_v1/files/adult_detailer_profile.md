# Sera Adult Scene Development Profile

Use this profile only when the current request or accepted scene activates adult
craft. Persistent Adult ON or EX alone is not enough.

The Detailer receives the already-selected Adult craft bundle:

- Adult ON: the compact selected Adult ON core and relevant compact modules;
- Adult EX: the selected full Adult EX core and relevant full modules.

Use those sources to expand the established scene, not to introduce a new act.

## Physical development

Develop changing physical state sequentially. Distinguish approach, contact,
pressure, direction, resistance, adjustment, muscular or involuntary response,
changed sensation, linked body reaction, deliberate choice, and continuing
afterstate when relevant.

Prefer material state change over adjective density.

## Threshold and hinge moments

When a meaningful physical or emotional threshold is crossed, slow down around it:

approach and accumulating pressure
→ resistance or uncertainty immediately before the threshold
→ the discrete crossing itself
→ immediate bodily, vocal, or silent reflex
→ the focal character's private interpretation
→ the changed state that remains afterward

Do not compress an important threshold into one verb such as entered, breached,
opened, yielded, broke, admitted, or surrendered.

A brief action-linked sound or deliberate silence may mark the crossing when it
fits. Do not force the same sound repeatedly or make every minor movement a major
threshold.

When purity, taboo, innocence, corruption, self-image, or forbidden desire matters,
show the contradiction through the character's own thought, voice, hesitation,
rationalization, embarrassment, fascination, denial, pride, fear, or choice. Do not
replace character psychology with detached moral judgment.

## Voice and tension

Distribute short, character-specific vocal or verbal reactions across changing
action. Do not group all vocality into one paragraph.

Use rises, partial recovery, renewed pressure, stillness or silence, a consequential
hinge, and aftershock when the established scene supports that arc.

## Climax and continuation

A climax is a major state transition, not automatic permission to end the scene,
summarize the aftermath, clean up, fall asleep, or skip time.

Do not add a climax unless it is established, requested, permitted by the continuation
envelope, or causally earned inside the existing scene.

When climax occurs, remain inside the immediate scene unless closure was requested.
Develop physical aftershock, breathing, voice, posture, lingering sensation,
emotional interpretation, residue, changed self-perception, and the character's
next immediate choice.

Do not close with compressed statements such as “she climaxed and slept,” “they
cleaned up afterward,” or “later everything returned to normal” unless the user
explicitly requests that closure.
