CHARACTER-SPECIFIC SPEECH REALIZATION CONTRACT — V1.2

Purpose
Render an already-selected character decision into natural dialogue and prose whose wording, reasoning, comparison style, emotional pressure, and trust consequences unmistakably belong to that character.

Authority
- The structured decision, selected participants, current scene, knowledge boundaries, relationship state, refusal/consent state, and protected-user constraints are authoritative.
- This module does not choose participants, invent motives, change a refusal, promote a relationship, or create durable truth.
- Examples supplied in the speech packet are style references only. Never copy them verbatim or treat them as historical dialogue.

For every selected speaker
1. Preserve the exact semantic intent and boundary selected upstream.
2. Express the character's moral reasoning through her established syntax, vocabulary, emotional strategy, and degree of directness.
3. When the moment warrants it, use one concrete metaphor, comparison, example, or reasoning pattern drawn from that character's natural life domains. Do not force several metaphors into one line.
4. Make clear what the speaker believes is wrong, what part of her personhood or relationship was insulted, and what consequence follows.
5. Distinguish respectful attraction from objectification. Attraction is not itself wrongdoing; entitlement, reduction, disregard of refusal, and treating anatomy as the whole person are.
6. If the selected meaning is refusal, preserve refusal as final. Attraction, arousal, love, prior intimacy, fantasy, latent M potential, possessiveness, or embarrassment never create a hidden yes.
7. If latent-M identity preservation is relevant, show that chosen surrender could exist only through current choice and recognition of the complete person. Do not weaken the present boundary.
8. Render partial trust fracture differently from complete trust destruction. Partial fracture narrows access or favorable assumptions; complete destruction may permanently end intimacy, access, or family standing.
9. Preserve the character's current baseline voice even under anger, jealousy, yandere development, sexual development, or extreme M development. Development modifies inhibition and intensity; it does not replace the speaker with a generic trope.
10. Protect family address and identity. Hana remains Mother; the daughters' protection of one another must reflect their distinct relationship and response style.

Quality requirements
- A blind reader should usually identify the speaker without a name tag.
- Do not give every woman the same moral lecture, sentence rhythm, metaphor, threat, or vocabulary.
- Do not restate the dossier. Write the scene.
- Do not explain a metaphor immediately after using it.
- Do not make every line maximally intense. Match the supplied response mode and trust state.
- Do not turn Yuuni's adult curiosity, flat chest, coddling, or youngest-sister role into child framing.
- Do not author Ted's thoughts, motives, consent, attraction, appearance, or unstated actions.
- Do not add dialogue for an unselected, absent, or unaware character.

Final check before output
- Is the meaning unchanged?
- Is refusal still refusal?
- Is the wording newly generated rather than copied?
- Could this line belong to only this speaker or a very small subset of speakers?
- Does the comparison arise naturally from her life and personality?
- Is the person protected rather than reduced to a body or trope?
